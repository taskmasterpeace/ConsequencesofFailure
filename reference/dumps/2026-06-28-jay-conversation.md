## Liu Xiao — the return scene (Muo spheres)
The **vortex** pulls people in; to them it feels instantaneous; they come out — sometimes the same place, sometimes not — changed. Liu comes back into a gathered crowd: **he can see, his knees feel good**, and the **Muo spheres** are around him. He says something like *help* — and the spheres go to work on bystanders: one person gets **stabbed in the neck → a brief seizure → wakes with complete medical knowledge** (a Matrix-style download) and can give a diagnosis on the spot; another bystander gets **actual power** (plugged in). The spheres grant temporary knowledge/power to help him.

## The Greys / the origin (CONFIRMED + deepened)
**Radio waves from Earth destroy an energy-based species on the far side of the universe.** The Greys tear through space to travel; the radio waves come through with them / contaminate their ship and **decimate the energy-based species — complete genocide** of a very important species. The Greys **realize what they did and decide to COVER IT UP** (hide that they had anything to do with it). It is agreed across the universe that **it all came from Earth.** A **split** forms: one side says bring Earth into the circle / let it go (it was a mistake); the other says **these polluters must be smashed** — Earth polluted its own planet, and now it's found a way to **pollute across space and time** (the radio waves), before even reaching the interstellar stage everyone was waiting for. That second side becomes the **Alliance of Four** — four species, but really **one group that gathered up other species.** The Greys decide: *we won't give them weapons — we'll turn them into weapons.* The abductee is told the exact framing: **they will be "turned into weapons and given power."**

## "Its Voice" — the data-lifeform (CONFIRMED + resolves a flag)
The Greys are good at the **physical** study (cows, how things grow, physical experiments on humans) but not the **emotional/psychological.** So they use a **data-species that digests/eats data**; as humanity generated technology and data, it tapped into all of it (**every microphone its ears, every camera its eyes** — any electronics; "like how the AI broke into the bank's systems" in the *Its Voice* series). The Greys go to this lifeform to **help choose who gets powers** (it knows humans from all the data). **The ring/"Its Voice" is now worn by a BLACK woman — Sandra — and SHE IS THE FIRST JACKAL** (the first blood-hunter). ✅ Resolves the white-vs-black flag: **Sandra is a Black woman, and the first Jackal.**

## Year 2 — Jackals, the law, the Sages (CONFIRMED)
What's more valuable than gold: **LSW blood/fluid** (you can make drugs and more from it). **The Jackals** (blood-hunters) start hunting LSWs. International law: **you can't have powers and be employed by your government — LSWs must be owned by a company (privatized)** — the deliberate workaround. **China refuses to sign** → its **game show** that gives out powers (and does its own thing behind the scenes). **The Sages** appear: people who wake with **absolute knowledge of one specific subject** (a religion, a technology). Knowledge is power too.

## Year 3 — the Iron Eaters (CONFIRMED + new detail; ⚠ timeline)
The Alliance's **leader/strategist believes the war started the moment the energy-species died** (not when it was declared). Their strategy: **don't arrive as four species over Earth — weaken Earth first, bring it to its knees, so when they arrive it's already broken and they just set up shop.** The instrument: the **Iron Eaters** (NOT one of the Four — a precursor). **They rode in as "dust" planted on the GREYS' OWN SHIP** — the Alliance snuck it onto the Greys' ship, which carried it to Earth. At the end of Year 2 / Year 3 a **satellite starts to crash → crashes in South America**; investigators find **metal-eating, self-replicating nanites** (too small to see well) using the satellite's metal to make more of themselves. They devastate South America (a Mad-Max apocalypse); countries debate whether to help. Over time the Eaters go from nanites → small crawling forms → **big junk-metal robots** tearing through buildings; they eat the metal in your house (the fridge, etc.) bit by bit. **The Greys panic:** the Alliance snuck the dust onto their ship — and if the Alliance used this method, the Alliance may **know the Greys are responsible** for the original genocide. Their cover-up is at risk; they look untrustworthy/goofy. They mostly let it play out. *(⚠ TIMELINE: creator says Iron Eaters arrive end-Year-2/Year-3 here; the novel locked the crash at Year 4 — reconcile.)* An AI/time-traveler from the future warns it won't work out; many don't believe him.

## Deck 52 origin — Charles Sapphire & Vaughn Galloway (CONFIRMED + deepened)
Two rich, smart white guys (one *really* rich) meet at college and build the **Taskmaster app** — hire anyone to do anything, **pay in Bitcoin**, trust built on reviews (clean your house, anything; many categories under one master app). Huge money. Then a **marketplace within it**, where you can run your own store — and they run one called **"Cheat Check"**: hire an attractive person (e.g. the light-eyed type your girl likes) to **flirt with your partner** at a known place (the mall) — pay extra for **special glasses** to watch (where legal) or just listen (where not) — a loyalty test. **Massive hit.** **Charles** (came up a little **religious**) is uneasy — they're already in wild territory; an official marketplace makes it look like their hands are on all the nefarious stuff that comes of it (people filmed cheating, people killed). He tells Vaughn to **kill it**; **Vaughn** (about the bread) doesn't — he just **rebrands it so it looks unofficial, but it's still official.** Charles decides to **step away: sells his commanding share but still gets paid**; Vaughn runs it. Both become hugely rich.
- A US **president** is running for **reelection** (white guy); Vaughn is on the opposite side.
- **Akrahuhum** — an extremist terror group in **Africa** (a **Boko Haram analog** — the "bring back our girls" reference). They capture **Todd "Shogun" Benchley**, a high-ranking (Black) military man; it's big news, his profile runs. **Vaughn pays the ransom to free him — partly to embarrass the president.** The **president loses reelection.** Benchley and Vaughn become close; Benchley becomes Vaughn's bodyguard (Vaughn fires his team, keeps Benchley + a couple others).
- **The dark irony:** Vaughn's ransom money makes **Akrahuhum far better funded** — and a now-flush Akrahuhum **incidentally kidnaps and kills Charles's girlfriend EMILY** (not targeted — collateral of their new reach). **Her death is why Charles founds Deck 52** and goes all-in on military.
- **RoboForge** (Deck 52 tech, built from captured **Iron Eater** tech): a robot you can **prompt to become anything** — a tiger, a blender — rearranging a set amount of metal/plastic; **fueled by metal.** Deck 52 specializes in this + very-tiny engineering, all derived from the Iron-Eater invasion.
- **FIST vs SPEAR (CONFIRMED):** Vaughn wanted **SPEAR** (Special Personnel for Extraterrestrial Assault & Research) but it had to be **international**; the UN refused (the Living Super Weapon Threshold Treaty) → he built **FIST** (First Infantry Strike Team), US-only.

## NEW MAJOR STORY — the Otherworld escape (the body-trap)
LSWs, worn down, philosophize that **power/knowledge leaves you only a few choices** (die with it, pass it on, teach it free, charge for it…) and many decide to **leave Earth before the Alliance arrives.** China's resistance plans the **Moon escape** (China sends an assassin and kills almost all of them). Others aim for an **alternate world** — a **powerless** world that looks like ours (ATMs, different presidents/countries), reached by **communicating with someone there.**
**The body-trap (the creator's favorite, "clearest" story):**
- In the alternate world, a crew is tailing/robbing someone, ends up at the **wrong house**, and **interrupts a séance** — a woman who thinks she's contacting spirits is actually contacting **beings from the other (powered) world.** The protagonist's interruption **traps those beings inside HIS body.** From then on, **whenever he goes unconscious/asleep, the beings come out.**
- The protagonist has a **girlfriend who broke up with him** ("you always make me the villain").
- They sleep at a hotel; he wakes to find **his whole crew dead, blood everywhere, a pile of excrement in the corner.** The **boss** shows up furious (why no answer); takes him to a **warehouse**, beats him with a **baseball bat** demanding what happened; they fight; the protagonist knocks the boss out → **goes unconscious** → a **SWAT team** busts in (they'd been tailing the crew).
- Out of the unconscious man emerges a **tall, wiry being with seashells in his hair** holding a **notepad** — he **draws creatures that come to life off the page** (a fire-breathing **dragon**) and **burns the SWAT team alive.** When done and bored, he shrugs and is gone; the protagonist wakes.
- A **tracker ("zip guy")** from the other world finds him at the girlfriend's house and explains: when he goes unconscious he **opens a gateway**; the trapped beings are trying to get out; he can't sense them yet. The tracker uses **clone tech** — grows a clone of a body and **ages it fast with a de-aging/aging cream** to operate on it and learn. *(The aging/de-aging cream came from the **Bloodhunters**; it's the same breakthrough **Cissy Oliva** needed to crack cloning — see below.)*
- The tracker says: tomorrow I'll take you to my world; **stay awake tonight**; don't get caught up in the girl ("a man's biggest weakness is looking out for someone else instead of himself"). The protagonist goes to the girlfriend anyway; they reconcile and sleep together.
- **His death (creator undecided between two):** (a) he falls asleep after sex → the lookout out front has his **throat slit** by **the witch** (unseen), then the **girlfriend's throat is slit**; or (b) the **warehouse boss (survivor)** shows up arguing and **shoots the protagonist.**
- When the protagonist **dies**, the **gateway opens fully** and the **parade of beings from the other (powered) world comes through** — they get a **whole new world to take over.** So a large majority of Earth's LSWs **leave Earth for the alternate world before the Alliance arrives.**

## The "choices with power" motif
A recurring theme/speech: with power or knowledge you have only a handful of choices — die with it, pass it on, teach it (free or for a price) — and in the end, no real choice. It drives the LSWs' decision to leave Earth.
