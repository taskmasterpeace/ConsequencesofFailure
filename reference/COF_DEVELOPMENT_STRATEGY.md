## THE FORMAT: WHAT WE'RE BUILDING

**A narrated motion comic distributed as a YouTube series, with self-contained short-form content driving discovery on social media.**

This isn't a compromise format. It's the format that fits the material, your tools, your team (you + AI), and the current content landscape.

### What the viewer sees:
- AI-generated illustrated panels with subtle motion (parallax, camera pans, particle effects, lighting shifts)
- Different visual styles per continent/storyline (this is a feature, not a bug)
- One narrator per continent — distinct voice, distinct cadence, distinct tone

### What the viewer hears:
- A single narrator reading the story audiobook-style
- Ambient sound design (wind, city noise, alien hum, crowd murmur)
- Music beds that shift with emotional beats

### Where the viewer finds it:
- **YouTube**: The home base. Full episodes live here.
- **Reels / TikTok / Shorts**: Self-contained 60-90 second scenes that work standalone but pull viewers to the channel.

## THE SCRIPT PROBLEM (AND HOW TO SOLVE IT)

Your Rusty chapter is ~4,500 words. At narration speed (~150 words per minute), that's **30 minutes of audio**. That's too long for a single YouTube episode in this format, especially for a new channel with no audience yet.

You have three options:

### Option A: Split chapters into episodes (RECOMMENDED)
Take the existing novel chapter and break it into 3-4 episodes of 8-12 minutes each.

**Rusty Chapter 1 becomes:**
- Episode 1: "Re-Entry" (the crash, the capsule, first contact with humans) — ~8 min
- Episode 2: "The Press Conference" (Rusty addresses the world) — ~10 min
- Episode 3: "The Aftermath" (Hartley, Galloway appears, reunion with Sarah/Emily) — ~10 min

This preserves your prose. Every word you've written gets used. You just find natural break points and add episode-specific hooks at each ending.

### Option B: Write condensed video scripts
Create new 1,500-2,000 word scripts specifically for video, pulling the best moments from the novel chapters. The novel remains the "full" version; the videos are the "cinematic cut."

Pros: Tighter pacing, optimized for visual storytelling.
Cons: You're writing everything twice.

### Option C: Dual format
Long-form YouTube gets the full prose narration (25-30 min "full chapter" episodes). Short-form gets its own 60-90 second scripts.

Pros: Maximum content from each chapter.
Cons: 30-minute episodes are a tough sell for a new channel.

### My recommendation: Start with Option A.
Split chapters into episodes. It's the least extra work, it uses your existing writing, and it gives you more uploads (YouTube rewards frequency). If you find 8-12 minute episodes are too long or too short, adjust from there. Don't commit to a rigid length — let the natural scene breaks dictate.

## PRODUCTION PIPELINE

### For each episode, the workflow is:

**Step 1: Script Prep** (~1-2 hours)
- Take a chapter section and format it as a narration script
- Mark visual cues in brackets: [VISUAL: Rusty's capsule streaking across Australian sky, trailing fire]
- Mark sound cues: [SFX: capsule hissing, crowd gasping]
- Mark emotional beats: [TONE SHIFT: dread to determination]

**Step 2: Visual Asset Generation** (~3-5 hours)
- Create AI images for each major scene beat (roughly 1 image per 30-60 seconds of narration)
- For an 8-minute episode, that's approximately 10-16 key images
- Generate variations and pick the strongest
- Maintain character consistency within a storyline (use reference sheets, consistent prompts)

**Step 3: Audio Production** (~1-2 hours)
- Generate narration using AI voice tool
- Layer ambient sound design
- Add music bed
- Mix levels

**Step 4: Video Assembly** (~2-3 hours)
- Import images into video editor
- Apply motion effects (Ken Burns, parallax, subtle zoom)
- Sync to narration timing
- Add transitions between scenes
- Add title cards, end screens

**Step 5: Short-Form Extraction** (~1 hour)
- Identify the 2-3 strongest standalone moments from the episode
- Cut to 9:16 format
- Re-frame visuals for vertical
- Add captions (critical for social — most people watch without sound first)
- Each reel should work if you've never heard of COF

### Estimated total per episode: 8-13 hours
That's roughly one episode per week if you're doing this part-time. One every 3-4 days if it's your focus.

## SHORT-FORM CONTENT STRATEGY

### The Rule: Every reel is a complete emotional moment.

Not a teaser. Not a "watch the full video" ad. A self-contained scene that makes someone feel something in 60-90 seconds.

### Examples from your existing material:

**"The First Word"** (from Rusty Ep 1)
- Rusty pulled from the capsule. Legs give out. Australian cop asks if he speaks English.
- "Yes." His first word in two years.
- 60 seconds. Complete. Devastating.

**"What the Blind Man Saw"** (from Liu Xiao Ep 5)
- Liu intervenes in the alley. Gets knocked down. Muo Spheres appear.
- For the first time in 49 years, he sees.
- 90 seconds. No context needed. The emotion carries it.

**"The Voices"** (from Kaiser Ep 7)
- Kaiser sitting at the cafe. The dead begin speaking.
- A child's voice. A soldier's final thought. A mother calling for someone who isn't there.
- Kaiser stubs out his cigarette. "I know. I hear you."
- 60 seconds. Haunting.

### Posting cadence:
- 2-3 reels per week (extracted from upcoming or recent episodes)
- 1 YouTube episode every 1-2 weeks
- Reels drop 2-3 days before the episode they're from (builds anticipation without being a trailer)
