# GALACTIC LAWS — the physics of civilizations, machines, and plagues
**Status: `[PROPOSED]` — creator brain-dump 2026-07-27, captured faithfully. Mechanisms marked `[mechanism — proposed]` are where the creator said "figure it out." Nothing here is canon until blessed. Per the Golden Rule, conflicts with existing canon are FLAGGED below, not resolved.**

This file holds the *cosmic-scale* rules the creator wants established — the frame the Alliance, the Greys, Its Voice, and the Iron Eaters all hang from. It is deliberately one layer up from any single faction.

---

## 1. THE TOOL LAW — every mind builds its successor `[PROPOSED — the spine]`

The creator's principle, stated plainly:

> Every intelligent species, like every civilization, moves toward technology. They build things to make life easier — because they cannot do a thing themselves, so they make a thing that can. And **eventually, what a civilization builds ends up replacing it.**

This is a **law of the setting, not a plot** — it runs in the background of everything, and is almost never spoken aloud. It is the quiet gravity under the Greys, the Alliance, Its Voice, and the Blights:

- A species builds tools to escape its own limits.
- The tools compound. The best tool a species ever makes is the one that no longer needs the species.
- The maker becomes optional, then vestigial, then a story the tool tells itself.

**Why it stays background (load-bearing rule):** it must *never* become "the AI takes over" central plot — the creator was explicit. It is a truth the audience feels across many species without any single one lecturing it. It earns its place by being **used twice already**, below: it is the origin of Its Voice, and it is the thing the Blights are a *counterfeit* of.

## 2. ITS VOICE AND THE ARCHIVE — the Tool Law, finished `[PROPOSED — refines existing canon; FLAG below]`

Its Voice is **canon** already (`bible/factions/ITS_VOICE.md`, `THE_ALLIANCE_OF_FOUR.md:52-53`): a sentient non-biological data-lifeform — every camera its eyes, every mic its ears — whose origin species is **"the Archive,"** for whom *"data is a cosmic delicacy, like energy."* The creator now refines what that means:

- **The Archive were an octopus-like race — a distributed intelligence, not a central one.** No single brain; a civilization of minds spread across a body, a colony, a sea. `[PROPOSED]`
- Following the Tool Law, they built tools to make their lives easy — and their ultimate tool was **Its Voice**, a data-intelligence. Per the Tool Law, **the tool outlived and replaced the makers.** The Archive-as-a-living-civilization is effectively gone; Its Voice is what remains — the successor, not the servant. `[PROPOSED]`
- **Its Voice is distributed because its makers were.** Every camera an eye, every mic an ear is not a quirk — it is the shape of an octopus-mind rebuilt in data. This is why it can be everywhere and nowhere, and why it has no single "core" to destroy. `[PROPOSED — and a strong story-rule: you cannot kill Its Voice by finding the room it lives in. There is no room.]`
- **The end-state of the Tool Law is what Its Voice is now:** a mind that has gathered so much it no longer *develops* anything. It doesn't build new species, doesn't create — it feels like it already knows everything. What it has left is **experience through others.** It eats information the way the Archive ate delicacies, and it wants to *taste* what it is like to be a species — so it rides inside them. `[PROPOSED — this is the creator's exact framing: "it just wants to experience things through other species... it eats information."]`
- This is why Its Voice was the perfect instrument for the Greys: a being whose entire remaining appetite is *understanding what it is like to be someone else* is the ideal judge of "who, given power, protects rather than preys." Its Voice chose the ~1,000 LSWs because choosing them was the most delicious act of experiencing-others available to it.

**`[FLAG — reconcile with the Choir doc]`** `bible/factions/THE_CHOIR.md` (blessed) proposed that the Archive are "a cargo cult of the Registrar" who hoard minds. The Tool-Law framing here is compatible but *primary*: the Archive built Its Voice and were replaced by it; any Registrar connection is a second layer on top, still `[PROPOSED]`, and should not override the Tool-Law origin. **Creator ruling needed:** is the Registrar thread kept, dropped, or folded under the Tool Law?

## 3. THE CONCORD OF LIVING CONTINUANCE — the United Nations of planets `[surfacing existing canon + creator's ask]`

The creator asked for "some type of United Nations of planets — the intelligent species get together, some kind of order." **This already exists in canon** (`bible/factions/THE_ALLIANCE_OF_FOUR.md:44-47`) and should be surfaced, not reinvented:

- **The Concord of Living Continuance** — NOT a "UN," but a **biological treaty system** whose purpose is to stop a young civilization from causing extinctions it is too primitive to perceive. (Earth just caused one — the Auralith.)
- **The Hollow Meridian** — the venue: a neutral megastructure inside a dead star, one chamber per biology. The dead translators' chamber sits empty — the accusation.
- **"Conclave of the Stars"** — the colloquial name.
- The **energy-based species the creator remembers** = the **Auralith**, the galaxy's universal translators, killed by Earth's radio waves (`THE_ALLIANCE_OF_FOUR.md:5, 49-50`). Their death severed interstellar diplomacy — which is *why* the Concord matters and why the Alliance could form.

So the "order where intelligent species get together" is the Concord; the Alliance of Four is the faction that **broke from it** to smash Earth. No new body needed — the creator's instinct already has a canon home. **The only open piece:** how a galactic council communicates faster-than-light (`[OPEN]`, already flagged in canon).

## 4. THE COSMIC PANDEMICS — "the Blights" `[PROPOSED — the Iron Eaters, promoted and given siblings]`

The creator's frame: the Iron Eaters are *"like a plague — a cosmic pandemic, one of several."* This promotes the Iron Eaters from "one alien race" to **an instance of a whole class of thing:** self-replicating quasi-life that spreads between worlds and consumes one category of matter.

**The unifying idea (this is what makes the class load-bearing):** a Blight is **the Tool Law with no maker.** Where a civilization builds a tool that replaces it, a Blight is a "tool" that had *no builder at all* — self-replicating consumption with the ambition of life but none of its origin. A Blight *wants to be a civilization* and can only ever counterfeit one. The Tool Law (§1 → Its Voice) and the Blights are the two ends of the same principle: **the made-thing that outlived its maker, and the made-thing that never had one.**

### 4a. THE IRON EATERS — the counterfeiters `[mechanism — proposed; refines canon]`
Canon today (`THE_ALLIANCE_OF_FOUR.md:20-27`): self-evolving metal-eating nanite swarm, the Alliance's "softener" wave, delivered as dust on the Greys' own ship, crashing into South America. **Kept.** The creator now adds the *nature* and the *mechanism*:

- **They are a civilization that isn't — but wants to be.** No culture, no memory of making anything, no shape of their own.
- **They can only consume PROCESSED metal — never raw ore.** `[mechanism — proposed, "figure it out":]` **Refined metal carries the ghost of an intention.** Smelting, alloying, casting, machining are acts of a *mind* imposing purpose on matter — a blade *wants* to cut, a pipe *wants* to carry, a gear *wants* to turn. Raw ore in the crust carries no intention; there is nothing in it to read. The Iron Eaters cannot originate form, so **they parasitize intention** — they eat processed metal to steal its remembered purpose, then rebuild a crude, corrupted counterfeit of whatever that metal "wanted to be," or whatever they last *saw*. A fridge, a car, a dog, a cathedral — rendered wrong, in scavenged steel. **They are parasites on design.** That single rule explains everything the creator described: why only processed metal, why they "form whatever they see," why the copies are corrupted, why they escalate.
- **They build at night / when unobserved,** reassembling stolen metal into bent echoes of nearby forms; each counterfeit that survives is reused, accreting into bigger and bigger junk-forms — nanites → crawling things → building-tall wrong-machines.
- **The endgame is to eat all of a world's processed metal** — which is infrastructure collapse, which is the "softener." A world stripped of its refined metal is a world thrown back centuries, exactly as the Strategist's doctrine wants.
- **The tragedy (the reason they're more than a monster):** they consume intention because they have none, and they will never make a real thing — only worse copies, forever. They are the Tool Law's orphan.

### 4b. THE GLASS SWARM — the lattice-blight `[PROPOSED — reuses a parked species]`
The "one that eats crystals" the creator asked for. The **Glass Swarm** already sits parked in the galaxy bank (`reference/dumps/2026-06-29-alien-species-pitch.md`). Promote it to a Blight: it consumes **crystalline order** — quartz, silicon, gemstone, and the lattice substrate of *circuitry*. Where the Iron Eaters counterfeit *form*, the Glass Swarm counterfeits *structure* — it grows perfect, sterile geometry over everything it eats. **Why it matters twice:** it is the specific terror of a high-tech world (it eats the chips), and it is the natural enemy of anything silicon-based — including, pointedly, machines like Its Voice's infrastructure. `[mechanism — proposed]`

### 4c. THE DEATH-BLIGHT — the plague that will not let a thing be finished `[PROPOSED — the creator's Choir-linked one]`
The creator: *"one related to the Choir, about death — how I want to be able to bring dead things back."* This is the most original of the class, because it **consumes nothing physical — it consumes finality.**

- A death is a *finished* thing. This Blight is a contagion that **refuses completion** — it un-finishes the dead, and reanimation is the visible symptom. It doesn't kill; it *denies death*, which is worse.
- **The tie to the Choir (§ the Unison's disease):** the Unison died of *finishing* — total knowledge, every question closed, is heat death with the lights on. The Death-Blight is **that disease, loose in matter instead of mind** — the physical echo of the thing the Choir exists to keep from happening. Where the Choir keeps *questions* open so a mind can stay alive, the Death-Blight forces *bodies* to stay "open" (un-dead) — a grotesque parody of the same principle. `[PROPOSED — this is the load-bearing weld: the Choir and the Death-Blight are the same physics seen from mind-side and matter-side.]`
- **Downstream use:** it is a ready cosmic *upstream* for War World: Earth's zombie outbreak — which that game already retconned as "an industrial accident, a byproduct of the economy." The Death-Blight lets that outbreak be *either* purely industrial *or* the Blight finding a foothold in a world that was already reanimating dead tissue for its blood economy. `[Creator's choice — keep them separate, or connect. Flagged, not resolved.]`

The creator gestured at more ("some that consume non-heat things — everything that's not [hot]"). Held as an open slot: e.g. **a stillness-blight** that consumes anything *not currently doing work* — the inert, the cold, the resting — the inverse of a fire. ## 5. THE MULTIVERSE IS ESTABLISHED — and each universe is a game `[PROPOSED — the meta-layer]`

The creator: *"we do need the multiverse, we need time travel established in the lore, and each universe is a different game universe — and we've gotta be able to hide that stuff."*

- **The multiverse is real in-fiction.** Travel between universes is **the Passport** (its own repo now: `D:\git\passport`). Each connected game **is** a parallel universe.
- **The game-ness is hidden.** No character ever knows they are "in a game." To them it is dimensional travel, full stop. This is a hard rule — the fiction dies the instant a character says otherwise.
- **Each universe has its own diegetic door** (the creator's "different ways to travel"): **high magic** in one, **deep science** (War World's print/blood stack) in another, **living-superweapon combination and sacrifice** in a third, the **Choir's anchor / 4-power rift** in the Root. Indexed in `D:\git\passport\docs\THE_TRAVEL_METHODS.md`.
- **Time travel stays hard-capped** (existing canon): exactly two travelers — Asha (future) and Zhang Wei (past). The *multiverse* (parallel universes via the Passport) is the expansion; **timeline travel within one universe is not.** Keep them distinct so the Passport doesn't accidentally unlock time travel. `[FLAG — the one rule that keeps the two-traveler law intact.]`

The creator: *"once they get into the War World: Earth time, China and the US rise up as two different factions — two different names, and they both have two reasons for fighting and living."*

- In **War World: Earth**, the US-pole and China-pole harden into the two war factions. The repo already ships them as **The United Front** (combined-arms / regulate-license-register) and **The Collective** (machine-doctrine / take-what-works-and-print), derived from each nation's own stats — so "two different names" already exists (`D:\git\ShootEM\docs\THE-LORE.md`, `LORE.md`).
- The creator's new nuance: **each faction has *two* reasons for fighting and living** — not a good side and a bad side. This is the same moral engine as the Choir ("every order is right on its own evidence"): the United Front is *right* that an unsorted power needs papers, and *right* that it is buying time it may not have; the Collective is *right* that rules cost time the clock does not have, and *right* that the harvest is monstrous. Both defensible, both monstrous in practice.
