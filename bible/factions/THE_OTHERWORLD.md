## Logline
With the **Alliance of Four** counting down to Earth, worn-down Living Super Weapons decide there is nothing left to do with their power but **leave** — and a crew in a powerless alternate world accidentally **traps a parade of powered beings inside one man's body.** When he dies, the door opens for good, and a large majority of Earth's LSWs walk out of the world before the Alliance ever arrives.

## The motif it belongs to — "choices with power"
A recurring speech/theme across the universe: with power or knowledge you have only a handful of choices — **die with it, pass it on, teach it (free or for a price)** — and in the end, no real choice at all. Exhausted by this, the LSWs stop fighting *for* Earth and start trying to **escape it before the Alliance lands.** (See `bible/VISION_AND_GOALS.md` for the power-system principles this rides on.)

## The two escape routes
1. **The Moon escape `[connection — separate arc]`.** China's resistance plans to flee to the **Moon**. China sends an **assassin** who kills almost all of them. *(Tracked here as a sibling arc; develop separately.)*
2. **The Otherworld (this story).** Others target an **alternate, powerless world** that looks like ours — **ATMs, but different presidents and countries** — reachable by **communicating with someone there.** The powered world and the powerless world can touch across that channel; that contact is the whole engine of the story.

## The world (the Otherworld)
A mundane mirror of Earth: recognizable infrastructure (ATMs, hotels, warehouses, SWAT) but a **different geopolitical layout** (other presidents, other countries) and, crucially, **no powers.** To its inhabitants, the powered world is the realm of "spirits" — which is why a **séance** is enough to brush up against it.

## The mechanism (the body-trap)
- A séance practitioner in the Otherworld believes she is **contacting spirits.** She is actually **contacting beings from the powered world.**
- A robbery crew, mid-job, follows their mark to the **wrong house** and **interrupts the séance** at the moment of contact. The interruption **traps those powered beings inside the protagonist's body.**
- Thereafter, **whenever the protagonist goes unconscious or asleep, the trapped beings come out** — his unconsciousness **opens a gateway** between worlds. He cannot sense them yet.
- **The full opening is death.** While he lives, the gate only cracks open when he is out. When he **dies**, the gateway **opens fully and permanently** — the parade comes through.

**The insight layer — cloning requires aging `[contact-science, in-world]`.** The tracker (below) operates via **clone tech**: he grows a clone of a body and **ages it fast with an aging/de-aging cream** so it is old enough to work on and learn from. The reason this matters is the thing the movies never explain — **you cannot clone without aging the clone.** A clone is born a baby and must grow; instant adult duplicates (the Superman/Bizarro problem) skip a step that cannot be skipped. The cream is the breakthrough that closes that gap. *(Same cream, same breakthrough, drives Cissy Oliva's cloning/animal-merging in Uganda — see Connections.)*
